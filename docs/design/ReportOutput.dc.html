Shade provision and elderly walking distance to transit

† Coverage note, carried from the pre-flight. The shade–distance link below rests on one peer-reviewed source and three press items. Nothing here is strong enough to carry a recommendation on its own. Climate context is drawn from work no newer than 2018.
What the evidence supports
Across the sources in scope, sheltered walking routes are consistently associated with longer accepted walking distances among residents over 65, but the size of the effect is not settled. The one peer-reviewed measurement in scope reports a shift of roughly 80–120 m in stated willingness; the government material describes the mechanism without quantifying it.
Thermal comfort is the mechanism every source proposes, and no source in scope proposes a competing one. That agreement is worth something, though it may reflect a shared literature rather than independent confirmation.

Where the sources disagree
Two sources conflict on whether the effect survives outside the immediate catchment. One reports no measurable change beyond 400 m from the stop†; the other treats the gradient as continuous. Both edges are kept in the graph; neither has been resolved.
